## disability-employment-uk- 
# Educational Attainment and Employment Outcomes for Disabled Adults in the UK
 
## Research Question
 
Does degree-level educational attainment correlate with employment outcomes for
disabled vs non-disabled UK adults, 2013–2021?
 
This is a cross-sectional, comparative, observational analysis, not an
experiment, and not a per-person longitudinal study. It compares group-level
rates over time, not individual outcomes.

## Methodology

The two datasets required cleaning and alignment before they could be compared. Disability-status labels were standardised so that both datasets used identical category names. An unweighted sample-size row was removed from the qualification data, as it did not represent a substantive estimate. The qualification data was organised according to the academic year periods (“July to June”) while the employment data is organised around quarters,  each employment quarter was mapped to the academic-year period in which it fell. The employment data was also restricted to the “In Employment” metric, excluding unemployment and economic-inactivity estimates not relevant to this analysis.

Following data preparation, the qualification and employment datasets were merged on academic-year period and disability status, producing a combined dataset. Pearson correlation coefficients were then computed between qualification-attainment rate and employment rate, separately for disabled and non-disabled adults. Two qualification levels were examined – “Degree or equivalent” and “No qualification” – as a robustness check, on the expectation that these two levels would show correlations of opposite sign if the underlying relationship were genuine rather than an artefact of a single variable choice.


## Data Sources
 
- **Table 1: Qualifications by disability status** — UK ONS/DWP, annual
  (academic-year) estimates of the percentage of disabled and non-disabled
  adults holding each qualification level, 2013–2021.
- **A08: Employment rates by disability status (GSS harmonised standard)** —
  UK ONS/DWP, quarterly employment rate estimates by disability status,

## Findings
 
**1. The employment gap between disabled and non-disabled adults did not
close over 2013–2021.** Employment rates rose for both groups, but the gap
between them held steady at roughly 30 percentage points throughout — from
about 44% (Disabled) vs 78% (Non-disabled) in 2013–14, to about 52% vs 81%
in 2020–21.
 
**2. Within each group, qualification and employment moved together.**
Degree-level attainment correlated strongly with employment within both
groups (Disabled: r = 0.929; Non-disabled: r = 0.774). "No qualification"
rates showed the mirrored, negative relationship (Disabled: r = -0.928;
Non-disabled: r = -0.737) — as the share with no qualifications fell,
employment rose, in both groups.
 
**3. Education tracked employment, but didn't close the
gap.** Disabled adults' own qualifications rose over the period, and their
employment rate rose alongside it — yet the gap with non-disabled adults
was no smaller at the end of the period than at the start. This suggests
that rising qualifications, on their own, are not sufficient to close the
disability employment gap. 

## Limitations
 
**Aggregate, not individual-level, data.** Every figure in this analysis is
a group percentage (e.g. "44% of disabled adults were employed"), not an
individual record. The correlations reported here are therefore ecological
correlations, they describe how group-level rates move together, not how
qualification and employment are related for any individual person. Robinson
(1950) demonstrated that ecological and individual-level correlations can
differ substantially in magnitude and even direction; the relationships
reported here should not be read as claims about individual-level causation.
 
**Small sample size.** Each within-group correlation is based on 8 annual
observations (2013–14 through 2020–21). A correlation coefficient computed
from 8 points is unstable, and should be treated as suggestive rather than
statistically robust.
 
**Shared time trend.** Both qualification attainment and employment rose
steadily across the study period, for both groups. Two variables that both
trend upward over time will tend to correlate strongly even without a
direct causal relationship between them; this analysis cannot fully rule
out that the observed correlations partly reflect this shared trend rather
than a specific link between qualification and employment.
 
**No disaggregation by severity, impairment type, or other demographics.**
This analysis compares only "Disabled" vs "Non-disabled" as a whole. UK
ONS/DWP data also permits disaggregation by impairment severity, impairment
type, sex, age, region, and country, factors known in the wider literature
to affect the size of the employment gap substantially (Office for National
Statistics, 2021). This analysis does not account for these factors.
